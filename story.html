Visitation After 2020

Among 372 Continuously Reporting NPS Units, Parks & Parkways Rebounded Above 2019 While Monuments & Historic Sites Lagged

The chart above tracks combined annual recreation visits for the 372 National Park Service units that reported attendance in every single year from 2015 through 2025, indexed so each category’s 2019 total equals 100.0. In 2020, all four categories declined, but by very different margins: Recreation Areas, Seashores & Parkways (43 units) edged down to 92.9 and National Parks & Preserves (75 units) fell to 75.3, whereas Historic Sites, Battlefields & Memorials (191 units) dropped to 53.8 and National Monuments (63 units) fell to 44.0. By 2021, both National Parks & Preserves (102.9) and Recreation Areas, Seashores & Parkways (101.2) had already exceeded their 2019 totals and remained above that benchmark in 2025 (103.8 and 101.8). In contrast, Historic Sites (86.3 in 2025) and National Monuments (86.2 in 2025) stayed below their 2019 baselines in every post-2020 year.

This split is worth examining because system-wide totals make post-2020 attendance look nearly flat: across these same 372 continuously reporting units, total annual visits moved from 325.0 million in 2019 down to 236.4 million in 2020, back up to 326.7 million in 2024, and to 314.1 million in 2025. Holding the set of reporting units constant shows that the near-return to 2019 aggregate volume is not a uniform recovery across the park system, nor an artifact of newly added park units—higher post-2020 totals at flagship parks and recreation corridors offset persistent shortfalls across more than 250 monuments, battlefields, memorials, and historic sites.

Data source and limitations: Figures come from National Park Service Visitor Use Statistics annual reports compiled in nps_annual_visits_1979_2025.csv. Three limitations apply to this comparison: first, restricting to the 372 continuously reporting units (out of 400 units active at some point during 2015–2025) keeps the panel consistent over time but excludes 28 newer or intermittently reporting units; second, unit categories are grouped from official NPS name suffixes; and third, annual visit counts are purely descriptive—they cannot separate changes in visitor demand from operational factors such as facility closures, construction, timed-entry rules, or counting-method updates, and they provide no information on monthly or seasonal visitation within a year.
